The future of phone calls

Relay is an OS-level layer that lets you hand your phone calls to the AI agent of your choice. It makes and takes the menial calls, keeps live and post-call transcripts, learns your context over time, and relays anything complex or sensitive back to you.

Switchboard is the universal protocol underneath. It lets any AI agent, from any developer, plug into Relay and be controlled from one set of settings. Together they imagine generative AI after the hype: not a novelty you open, but infrastructure you stop noticing, like a dial tone.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Menial phone calls are a chore nobody wants but everybody needs.

How might we integrate AI into the phone call, without making the human disappear from it?

Demo

AI as infrastructure

AI is here to stay, but it won't always be exciting. Relay looks past the peak of the hype, to the point where personal AI agents become another digital tool we live with but never think about.

Relay is what that future looks like, and it is already on the horizon. Switchboard is how we will control it. If it works, it will feel like nothing ever happened.

The phone call, reinvented

  1. 01

    1994

    Make and take calls

    IBM Simon, the first smartphone, put calls in your pocket. It was bulky, and static could drown you out.

  2. 02

    2007

    One button to call

    The first iPhone let anyone be heard clearly and instantly, at the touch of a button.

  3. 03

    Next

    A third option

    People want to be heard without their voices being heard. Beside Call and End Call sits a third choice: relay it.

Make AI boring.

The most successful technology disappears into everyday language: we google things, we text people. Relay is designed the same way, so that one day you simply relay a call.

Relay

A personal social task agent built into your phone. Not an app, a chatbot or another assistant, but an integrated service layer that makes annoying phone calls disappear.

  1. 01

    Makes calls, takes calls

    Appointments, reservations, RSVPs, rescheduling, deliveries and simple inquiries, handed off with one button.

  2. 02

    Text or voice, your choice

    Tell Relay what you need by typing or speaking, and it carries the conversation for you.

  3. 03

    Live and post-call transcripts

    Follow the call as it happens, step in mid-call, or read the full transcript afterwards.

  4. 04

    Knows when to hand back

    Payments and complex or emotional calls are relayed back to you. It's in the name.

Relay in use

Who it's for

Anyone who can't take or make a call at a given moment, especially people with social anxiety or telephobia, people who dislike menial tasks, people with little free time, and people with speech impairments.

Built-in limits

Relay handles menial calls on its own, but it will never be authorized to disclose payment information, access personal data you haven't permitted, or clone voices.

Switchboard

If Relay is the faucet, Switchboard is the plumbing behind it: a universal protocol that lets AI voice agents from any developer take on calls through Relay, under one set of controls.

The plumbing

Relay and Switchboard are not two separate products. Relay is the consumer product, and Switchboard is the disruptor behind it, built from an API bridge, an agent SDK, a consent registry, a privacy ledger and a handshake between agents.

Why now

Companies are starting to notice that people no longer want to make phone calls. As of April 2026, Google Duplex, OpenAI's Realtime API and a wave of startups offer AI agents that can call for you, and high-fidelity agents are expected by 2027. None of them treat AI as infrastructure, or plan for how people will control it.

The trust gap

The people who stand to gain the most from AI calling are skeptical of it, because no way of controlling it exists yet. Switchboard solves this, and Relay is the proof of concept. So how do we control something designed to be universal?

By doing what we're best at: pressing buttons and flipping switches.
